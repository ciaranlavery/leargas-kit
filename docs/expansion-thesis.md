# Expansion thesis: history compounds

Every subscribed month does two things at once: it pays again for work already
done, and it lengthens the time series that future extras are built from.
Retention and expansion become one motion. Churn gets costlier for the client,
because no competitor can reconstruct their history on day one.

## Product ladder by data age

| Age | Unlocks | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Week 1 | Base pack: point-in-time numbers, wobble-prone week-on-week deltas | First Monday numbers |
| Months 2–3 | Hardened baselines, measured normal ranges | Promo verdicts citing same-weekday history; variance bands replace gut feel |
| Quarters 2–4 | Seasonal shape | Staffing and promos around Christmas, summer, tournament cycles; cash forecast with seasons in it |
| Year 2+ | Structural evidence | Year-on-year truth, lease-renewal packs, staffing-model rebuilds, multi-site comparison |
| Portfolio (N clients × time) | Anonymized benchmarks | "Cafés your size do 18% of revenue Saturday; you do 31%" — uncopyable without the installed base |

## Rules

1. **Retain every nightly pull immutably.** History is architectural, not a
   byproduct — without it, nothing above month 1 exists.
2. **Sequence extras by data age in the sales motion.** Never sell seasonality
   to a month-one client; thin verdicts get blamed on the product.
3. **Every extra obeys the build-once rule:** capped build hours, weekly run
   with zero touches; unknown inputs become new rows, never broken packs,
   never config tickets.
4. **6-month post-exit purge stands**, with aggregate-anonymized learnings
   explicitly carved out — which requires the benchmark consent wording in the
   client agreement from day one.

## Open (wayfinder fog)
